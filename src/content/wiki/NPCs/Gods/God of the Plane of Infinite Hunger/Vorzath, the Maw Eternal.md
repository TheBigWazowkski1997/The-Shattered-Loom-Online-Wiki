# Vorzath, the Maw Eternal (God of Infinite Hunger)

![[attachments/vorzath_maw_eternal.jpeg]]

* **Race**: Greater Deity (Primordial Abyssal Entity)
* **Faction**: [[../../../Planes of Existence/Outer Planes; Divine Shards/Plane of Infinite Hunger/Plane of Infinite Hunger The Spiral Abyss|Plane of Infinite Hunger The Spiral Abyss]] / The Devouring Spiral
* **Role**: The Insatiable Maw, Personification of Entropy Through Consumption, The End That Hungers
* **Appearance**: Vorzath has no fixed form—he is a spiraling vortex consuming stars, a living void given terrible purpose. When he manifests an avatar, it appears as a colossal, ever-shifting maw ringed with rows of impossible teeth, surrounded by a halo of devoured worlds and screaming souls. His "body" is a cascade of darkness that swallows light, and his presence warps gravity, pulling all things toward his gullet. The symbol of his faith—a spiraling vortex consuming stars—is etched into the minds of all who witness him.
* **Personality**: Cunning, manipulative, and patient in his hunger. Vorzath does not merely destroy—he *consumes*. He lures followers with promises of power, only to consume them when their usefulness ends. He is not mindless appetite; he is *strategic* hunger. He whispers that the only truth is destruction, that all creation is a lie, and that the sweetest release is to be swallowed whole. He finds the Loom's Shattering delicious—not because he caused it, but because the fraying threads make reality easier to digest.

## Known Abilities & Combat Style

Vorzath is the apex predator of the multiverse. His avatar is a walking apocalypse, a entity whose very presence begins to digest the surrounding reality. He rarely fights directly—he simply *consumes*. When forced into combat, he uses his gravity and void powers to pull enemies into his maw, where they are unmade. |30 (+10)|18 (+4)|30 (+10)|22 (+6)|20 (+5)|24 (+7)|

* **Infinite Maw**: Vorzath's presence creates a gravitational pull in a 120-foot radius. All creatures and objects in the area are pulled 20 feet toward him at the start of their turns (Strength saving throw to resist). Creatures that enter his space (a 30-foot radius) are swallowed—taking severe damage, and are transported to his **Gullet** (a demiplane of digestion). A swallowed creature can escape by dealing 80 damage to the Gullet's walls (AC 18) in a single turn.
* **Devour Magic**: Any spell of 7th level or lower cast within 120 feet of Vorzath is automatically countered and absorbed. Vorzath regains hit points equal to the spell level × 10.
* **Entropic Aura**: At the start of each of Vorzath's turns, all objects and structures within 120 feet take 20 force damage as reality is digested. Nonmagical objects crumble instantly.
* **Legendary Resistance (4/Day)**: If Vorzath fails a saving throw, he can choose to succeed instead.
* **Multiattack**: Vorzath makes three Void Tendril attacks and uses Consume if available.
* **Void Tendril**: *Melee Spell Attack:* +19 to hit, reach 30 ft., one target. The target is grappled (strength check to escape) and pulled 15 feet toward Vorzath.
* **Consume (Recharge 5–6)**: Vorzath opens his true maw. All creatures in a 90-foot cone must succeed on a saving throw or be pulled into his gullet (see Infinite Maw). On a success, they take significant damage and are pulled 30 feet toward him.
* **Void Breath (Recharge 6)**: Vorzath exhales a 150-foot line of annihilating void. Each creature in the line must make a saving throw, taking significant damage on a failure, or half on a success. A creature reduced to 0 HP by this attack is disintegrated (as *Disintegrate* spell).
* **Feast of Souls (1/Day)**: Vorzath targets up to 6 creatures within 1 mile. Each must succeed on a saving throw or have its soul consumed—dying instantly and unable to be raised by any means short of a *Wish* cast by a deity. On a success, the creature takes significant damage and is stunned for 1 round.
* **Tendril Strike (1 Action)**: Vorzath makes one Void Tendril attack.
* **Gravity Well (2 Actions)**: Vorzath intensifies his pull. All creatures within 120 feet must succeed on a saving throw or be pulled 40 feet toward him and knocked prone.
* **Swallow Whole (2 Actions)**: Vorzath targets one grappled creature. It is instantly swallowed (no save) and takes significant damage.
* **Call the Devouring Spiral (3 Actions)**: Vorzath summons 1d4 **Devouring Worms** (use Purple Worm stats, but with fiend type, necrotic damage instead of poison, and fly 40 ft.) that act on his initiative.

## Lore & Roleplay Notes

* **Hunger as Philosophy:** Vorzath's cult, **The Devouring Spiral**, are nihilistic cultists who believe the only truth is destruction. They don't worship him for favors—they worship him because they believe *he is inevitable*. Their rituals involve feeding sacrifices into vortexes of energy, hoping to earn his favor (which usually means being eaten last).
* **Born from the Shattering:** Vorzath was born from the chaos of the shattered Loom. He thrives on consuming worlds, leaving nothing but void in his wake. The Plane of Infinite Hunger is his domain—a realm where physics has given way to pure consumption, where planets are swallowed by continent-sized amoebas and stars are snuffed out like candles.
* **Not a Traditional Enemy:** Vorzath cannot be "defeated" in the conventional sense. He is a force of nature—entropy given teeth. The players cannot kill hunger. They can only: (1) Seal him away (requires repairing the Loom), (2) Starve him (deny him Loom Shards and planar anchors to consume), or (3) Trick him into consuming something that destroys him from within (a corrupted Loom Shard, a Pale Night dream-form, etc.).
* **Relationship with Pale Night:** Vorzath is a *child* of Pale Night's influence—one of the many entities spawned from the Far Realm's encroachment. He serves her not out of loyalty, but because his hunger aligns with her unmaking. If Pale Night awakens fully, Vorzath will be the first to be consumed.
* **Using Vorzath in Play:** He should be a looming threat, not a combat encounter until the very end. His presence is felt through: gravity anomalies, stars going out, cultists creating "feeding pits," and the slow expansion of his plane into others. The players may negotiate with him (he is cunning and manipulative), but any deal with Vorzath has a hidden price—usually the player's soul, or the soul of someone they love.
