# Elder Oakhaven (Grand Caliper of Sap & Sinew)

![[attachments/elder_oakhaven.jpg]]

* **Race**: Firbolg (Verdant Shaper)
* **Faction**: [[../Factions/The Consortium of the Gilded Caliper|The Consortium of the Gilded Caliper]]
* **Role**: Grand Caliper of Sap & Sinew — Timberforge Representative of the Triumvirate
* **Appearance**: A massive, moss-bearded firbolg standing over eight feet tall. He wears simple robes made of living vines and leaves that blossom and fade in response to his mood. A wooden staff tipped with a glowing emerald rests in his hand at all times. His bark-like skin is ancient and gnarled, and his slow movements carry an immovable sense of gravity.
* **Personality**: Patient, soft-spoken, and deeply connected to the natural rhythms of the plane. He speaks slowly but carries the unyielding authority of a mountain. He finds Hesperus Vane's obsession with permanence philosophically offensive — everything must eventually decay and return.

---
## Tactical Profile & Combat Demeanor

> [!NOTE]
> **Dungeon Master Directive**: Complete numeric statistics, Armor Class, Hit Points, spell slots, and action mechanics are restricted to the Dungeon Master's Master Vault.

Elder Oakhaven is a formidable nature-warden who binds enemies in vines, channels the raw force of the forest, and regenerates in the presence of living earth. He does not fight unless there is no other option — but when he does, it is like fighting a landslide.

---

## Lore & Roleplay Notes

* **The Anti-Vane:** His entire political agenda is preventing Hesperus Vane from mechanizing the Timberforge forests. He views every clockwork construct as a scar on the living plane and will oppose automation legislation at every turn.
* **The Long View:** Oakhaven never rushes. He will listen to a player's entire speech, wait three seconds of silence, then respond with a single, measured sentence that cuts to the heart of the matter. He speaks in metaphors drawn from forestry and seasons.
* **Trusting the Players:** If the players respect his philosophy (even partially), he becomes a slow but reliable ally. He sees them as "young saplings" who need soil, time, and the right weather. He will not help them break the Loom — he believes the fracturing is the universe's way of pruning itself.
